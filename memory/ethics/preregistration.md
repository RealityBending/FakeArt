Study Description:

This follow-up study investigates (1) the effect of individuals’ prior aesthetic appraisals (beauty, emotional valence, meaningfulness, worth) on the subsequent recognition and memory of artworks and their associated prior subjective reality beliefs (Human vs AI-Generated and Original vs Copy/Forgery); (2) the impact of attitudes towards AI on participants’ recognition accuracy of artworks according to a prior reality beliefs modulation (Original/AI-Generated/Human Forgery)...... [ADD & ADJUST ACCORDING TO METHODOLOGY CHANGES]. Participants are shown 96 artworks (all human-made originals, extracted from the VAPS dataset), including images seen in the previous study as well as novel images. Each artwork is presented separately, where all participants are firstly asked to rate the perceived beauty of the artwork (from "Ugly" to "Beautiful") as well as to what extent the painting is personally relevant to them on a 7-point scale (from "Not at all" to "Very much"). After, the participant is asked whether they recognise the stimulus from the previous study: (1) if “Yes”, participants must state which label the image was assigned in the previous study as per the reality beliefs modulation (Original/AI-Generated/Human Forgery) and are asked to remember their subjective reality beliefs about the stimulus (Human Original/Human Forgery/AI Original/AI Copy); (2) if “No”, participants are only further asked to rate the perceived AI-likeness of the artwork (from "Not at all" to "Very much"). All code, content, timeline, and evolution of the experiment are publicly available at https://github.com/RealityBending/FakeArt
